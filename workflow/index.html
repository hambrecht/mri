R workflow: density surface models for aerial ungulate surveys

Author

Leonard Hambrecht, IRSS, University of British Columbia

Published

September 1, 2026

Introduction

This series of articles walks through a complete distance sampling analysis of an aerial survey in R: from raw sightings and flight lines to maps of density and estimates of abundance with their uncertainty. The results are then used to simulate and compare designs for future drone surveys. It uses real data from a 2025 thermal-drone survey of moose, elk, deer, bison and wolves in Riding Mountain National Park, Manitoba.

The articles are written in the same style as the dsm and dsims package articles on distancesampling.org, and link to them where they cover the general method. They add the steps those examples leave out: turning flight tracks into segments, extracting covariates from raster layers, building a prediction grid, and fitting species-specific models to a multi-species survey following Roberts et al. (2016).

The workflow

Article What it does Main packages
Formatting aerial survey data Reads sightings and flight lines, computes perpendicular distances, chooses the truncation distance, cuts transects into segments and builds the detection, segment and observation tables sf
Adding covariates and building a prediction grid Extracts lidar canopy cover and height at sightings, over segment strips and over a 500 m prediction grid terra, exactextractr
Estimating the detection function Fits half-normal and hazard-rate detection functions with species and canopy covariates and selects one by AIC Distance
Fitting a density surface model Fits habitat and spatial DSMs per species, checks them and assesses extrapolation dsm, dsmextra
Estimating abundance and its uncertainty Predicts density, estimates variance, maps uncertainty and tabulates abundance for all species dsm
From density surface model to simulation Converts the moose DSM and detection function into the region, density, population and detectability objects of dsims dsims
Survey designs with subplots Generates subplots sized for one drone flight, places systematic and zigzag transects inside them, and compares coverage and effort with a design across the whole park dsims, dssd
Simulating surveys with spatially varying detectability Simulates surveys in which detectability depends on canopy cover at each animal, and compares designs and analyses dsims (fork)

The first five articles are the core analysis. The last three use its results to test survey designs by simulation and can be skipped if you only need estimates from your own survey.

Each article ends by saving its results to workflow/outputs/, and the next one starts by loading them. You can therefore start at any article, as long as the files from the previous step exist.

All the code of the five core articles is also available as a single script, rmnp_workflow.R. It includes the data check functions, and it is the easiest starting point for analysing your own survey: replace the file names and column names in the first section, run the checks, and work through the script. The simulation articles are kept separate, because they are only needed when planning a new survey.

Getting started

Download the example data, rmnp_example_data.zip (22 MB), and unzip it into a data/ folder in your project. The first article also downloads it automatically if it is missing. The zip contains RMNP/:

  • RMNPobservations.csv: sightings with coordinates, species and group size;
  • SimpliedFlightTrack.shp: flight lines on effort;
  • RMNPArea.shp: the study area boundary;
  • lidar/lidar_pabove2_zmax_30m.tif: lidar canopy cover and canopy height.

The code uses here() for file paths, so run it from an RStudio project, or any folder that contains a .here file or a Git repository.

To use your own data, you need the same four ingredients: sightings with coordinates, species and group size; the lines flown on effort; a study area polygon; and covariate rasters covering the study area.

Packages

Most packages come from CRAN:

install.packages(c("Distance", "mgcv", "tweedie", "sf", "terra", "exactextractr",
                   "dplyr", "units", "ggplot2", "here", "remotes"))

Four packages are installed from GitHub, from forks maintained for this workflow. The forks are based on the development versions in the DistanceDevelopment repositories rather than the CRAN releases. The CRAN releases of dsims and dssd date from April 2025, and the development versions include changes made since then. The table lists what each fork adds on top of the development version.

Package Fork Changes from the development version Needed for
dsm hambrecht/dsm Speed improvements to dsm_var_gam() and to summary() of variance objects. Results are identical to the original. Density surface model, Abundance (optional)
dsmextra hambrecht/dsmextra Removes the dependency on the retired rgdal package and a deprecated dplyr function. dsmextra is not on CRAN; these changes are also merged into the original repository. Density surface model (required)
dssd hambrecht/dssd Parallel run.coverage(), and much faster transect generation and coverage grids. Results are identical to the original. Survey design and simulation (optional, speed only)
dsims hambrecht/dsims New: detectability that varies with a raster covariate at each animal’s location (cov.surface in make.detectability()). Also faster parallel simulations. Simulation with spatially varying detectability (required)

Install them in this order, so that dsims finds the forked dssd:

remotes::install_github(c("hambrecht/dsm", "hambrecht/dsmextra",
                          "hambrecht/dssd", "hambrecht/dsims"))

Alternatively, the exact package versions used to build these articles are recorded with renv in the website repository. After cloning it, renv::restore() from the repository root installs all of them, including the forks.
