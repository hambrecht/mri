Best Practice Guide

This guide distills our simulation and deep learning research into practical recommendations for planning drone-based moose surveys. Each recommendation links to the full study behind it.

1. Choose Your Platform

Scenario Recommended Platform & Design Evidence
Large area (> 10 × 10 km), any density Fixed-wing drone with a zigzag transect design and line-transect distance sampling Multi-platform Simulations
Small, accessible, or rugged area (≤ 10 × 10 km), density > 400 individuals/1,000 km² Quadcopter with 0–10% overlap strip transects, 9–16 sample blocks Quadcopter Simulations
Small, accessible, or rugged area, density < 400 individuals/1,000 km² Quadcopter with 65% overlap census, carefully reviewed to remove double-counted individuals — or reconsider a fixed-wing platform Quadcopter Simulations

Helicopters remain a robust baseline with the best coverage, but they are expensive, carry observer safety risk, and tend to underestimate density. Fixed-wing drones were the most accurate alternative across all tested densities. (Multi-platform Simulations)

2. Design Your Survey

  • Use zigzag transects for fixed-wing drones. They achieved up to 96.6% of flight time on-transect, compared to 80.5% for the helicopter’s segmented grid design. (Multi-platform Simulations)
  • Avoid the 200 m fixed-spacing quadcopter design. It consistently overestimated population size and failed to meet the precision threshold at any density tested. (Quadcopter Simulations)
  • Match sample block count to required precision. Quadcopter surveys needed 9–16 sample blocks at moderate-to-high densities, and all 25 blocks for the gimbal line-transect design to meet the CV ≤ 0.15 precision threshold. (Quadcopter Simulations)

3. Configure Cameras & Sensors

  • Avoid nadir-only camera configurations. A straight-down camera assumes uniform detection within a narrow strip and significantly overestimates density. (Multi-platform Simulations)
  • Equip quadcopters with a gimbal or dual-camera setup when a wider, angled field of view is needed — this brings quadcopter estimates much closer to fixed-wing accuracy. (Multi-platform Simulations)
  • Use thermal infrared sensors for nighttime or low-visibility surveys. Thermal imagery mitigates visual occlusion and enables detection outside daylight hours. (Deep Learning)

4. Process & Review Imagery

Manual review of drone imagery is time-consuming, inefficient, and error-prone at scale. A multi-stage deep learning framework — first identifying candidate hotspots, then confirming species — automates detection and classification of moose and other terrestrial mammals from thermal footage, making large imagery volumes practical to review. (Deep Learning)
